How a login runs

One authorization-code exchange with PKCE. Five steps, real endpoints.

1

Your app sends the user to authorize.

A redirect with a PKCE challenge and a random state.

GET /api/auth/oauth2/authorize
  response_type=code
  scope=openid
  client_id=<your client>
  redirect_uri=<exact registered URI>
  code_challenge=<S256 challenge>
  code_challenge_method=S256
  state=<random>
2

The user signs in on your screen.

Your login screen, your brand. Authward checks the credential.

screen       yours
credential   passkey, email link or social
checked by   Authward
3

Authward returns a code.

To the exact redirect URI you registered, with the issuer attached.

302 <redirect_uri>
  code=<authorization code>
  state=<same random value>
  iss=https://auth.olaryn.com
4

Your backend trades the code for tokens.

The PKCE verifier proves it is the same client.

POST /api/auth/oauth2/token
  grant_type=authorization_code
  code=<authorization code>
  code_verifier=<PKCE verifier>
  client_id=<your client>
  redirect_uri=<same URI>

200 OK
  access_token   JWT, valid 15 minutes
  id_token       signed with EdDSA
5

You verify the token and start your session.

Standard JWT checks against the published keys.

iss   = https://auth.olaryn.com
aud   = <your client or API>
sig   = key from jwks_uri
keys rotate every 30 days

Setup

Paste one prompt into your coding agent.

  1. 1

    Copy the prompt from your workspace. It holds a workspace ID and no secrets.

  2. 2

    Approve in your browser. Access covers one workspace for 24 hours and can be revoked.

  3. 3

    Your coding agent does the rest. It registers the app, environments and OAuth client, then verifies discovery and the signing keys.

Also available as a CLI and an MCP server. Agents can read the integration guide.
